East of Eden

John Steinbeck, 1952

I've always wanted to start a reading club, and East of Eden has been at the top of my list for a long time. So many readers have told me it's the best book they've ever read. I'd love to take that journey together with friends, trading perspectives and thoughts along the way, so it becomes something we share.

Fewer than ten of us, a Zoom every two weeks, and dinner together in New York once we've all turned the last page. I'll prep discussion questions and a few materials for every check-in, so all you need to bring is the reading.

The book

Why this one

Many readers call it their all-time favorite, or simply the best book they've ever read.

  • No. 1Topped the New York Times fiction bestseller list within two months of its release in 1952.
  • His big oneSteinbeck considered it his most important book, and the hardest he ever wrote.
  • NobelSteinbeck went on to win the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1962.
  • OprahPicked for Oprah's Book Club in 2003, which introduced it to a whole new generation.
  • 70+ yearsNever gone out of print, and still sells about 50,000 copies a year.
  • On screenBecame the 1955 Elia Kazan film starring James Dean, with four Oscar nominations. Save it for after we finish.

No need to have read any Steinbeck before, and no homework beyond the reading. Come as you are.

How it works

A few house rules

I

No spoilers, ever

On calls and in the group chat, we only talk about what's been assigned so far. Reading ahead is totally fine, just keep it to yourself.

II

Skip the introduction

A lot of editions open with an essay that gives away the story. Save it, and any online summaries or reading guides, for after you finish.

III

Go by chapters

Page numbers differ between editions, so every checkpoint is a chapter number. Paperback, ebook, library copy and audiobook all count.

IV

Behind? Still come

Life happens. If you're a few chapters short, join anyway. You'll just hear a little more than you've read.
