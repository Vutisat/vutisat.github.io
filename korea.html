Day 1 Sunday, Dec 20 Arrival & Myeongdong lights
2:05 pm
Land at Incheon & check in
Arrive at ICN, clear immigration, transfer into the city and drop bags before heading out.
+ Getting around

Grab a T-money card at any convenience store and tap onto the spotless subway for everything. Airport → city is easiest on the AREX train (~43 min direct to Seoul Station) or a KakaoTaxi (~₩60–70k, 70 min). It’ll be cold — December in Seoul runs around -5°C to 3°C, so pack a proper coat on top.

Late afternoon
Myeongdong
Neon shopping streets strung with Christmas lights — skincare, socks and the city’s most famous street-food lane.
+ Photo spot — the Christmas display

The giant illuminated tree and light tunnels around Myeongdong Cathedral and the Shinsegae / Lotte department-store facades are Seoul’s most over-the-top winter displays — go after dark when they’re fully lit.

+ Don’t miss — the street-food lane

Graze your way down the main drag: gooey tornado potato, cheese-pull corn dogs, gyeranppang (egg bread), grilled cheese lobster tails and torched wagyu skewers. Great cheap dinner-on-your-feet on arrival night.

Evening
Namsan Seoul Tower
The city’s hilltop landmark — ride the cable car up for the glittering night skyline.
+ Photo spot — the night skyline

The observation deck gives you Seoul lit up in every direction. For the tower itself, shoot from the base plaza looking up, or catch it framed above the trees on the walk up from Myeongdong.

+ For two — the love locks

The terraces below the tower are covered in thousands of padlocks left by couples. Bring one (they’re sold on-site), clip it on and keep the key — a classic Seoul date-night thing to do.

Dinner
Myeongdong KyojaFOOD
Institution near the shopping streets — one menu, done perfectly: hand-cut kalguksu noodles and fat mandu (dumplings).
+ Warm-up alternative

Freezing? Duck into any tteokbokki spot for chewy rice cakes in spicy-sweet gochujang sauce, or a bowl of sundubu jjigae (soft-tofu stew) — the ultimate cold-weather comfort food.

Day 2 Monday, Dec 21 Palaces & hanok villages
Morning
Gyeongbokgung Palace
Seoul’s grandest Joseon-era palace — catch the changing of the royal guard ceremony at the main gate (usually 10am & 2pm).
+ Insider tip — wear a hanbok, get in free

Rent a hanbok (traditional dress) from any shop by the gate and palace entry is free — and you’ll get the best photos of the trip. Closed Tuesdays, so today (Monday) is perfect.

+ Photo spot — Gyeonghoeru Pavilion

The pavilion on stilts over the pond is the classic frame; also line up the throne hall (Geunjeongjeon) straight through the gates. In winter the bare-tree-and-tiled-roof look is stark and beautiful.

Late morning
Bukchon Hanok Village
A living neighbourhood of restored traditional houses on the hillside between the palaces — quiet lanes and tiled rooftops.
+ Photo spot — Bukchon-ro 11-gil

The famous sloped alley looking down over hanok rooftops toward the city (and Namsan Tower on a clear day) is the shot. Please keep your voice down — people actually live here, and visits are limited after 5pm.

+ Hidden gem — Jeongdok Library viewpoint

Skip the crowds at the Gahoe-dong alleys and head to the slightly raised terrace by Jeongdok Public Library for a calm panorama of rooftops backed by the mountains.

Lunch
Tosokchon SamgyetangFOOD
Just west of Gyeongbokgung — Seoul’s most famous samgyetang (whole young chicken stuffed with rice & ginseng in a milky broth). Perfect winter warmer.
+ Nearby alternative — Seochon

The Seochon village on the palace’s west side is quieter than Bukchon and packed with cosy lunch spots and cafes — a lovely low-key wander if the queue’s too long.

Afternoon
Ikseondong
Seoul’s oldest hanok quarter, reborn as narrow alleys of trendy cafes, boutiques and pubs tucked inside old courtyard houses.
+ Hidden gem — a hanok cafe stop

Warm up over coffee in a courtyard: Cheong Su Dang (bamboo-lined, gorgeous), or Ikseon-dong’s dessert cafes for a fluffy soufflé pancake. Quieter and more atmospheric than Bukchon.

+ Photo spot — the lantern alleys

The narrow lanes strung with paper lanterns and glowing shopfronts photograph beautifully once the light drops.

Evening
Cheonggyecheon Stream
A restored stream running right through downtown — a peaceful walk below street level.
+ Photo spot — the winter lanterns

Around the holidays the stream hosts glowing lantern displays along the water. Start at the big illuminated spiral (Spring sculpture) near Gwanghwamun and stroll east as everything reflects off the stream.

Day 3 Tuesday, Dec 22 DMZ & a Korean spa
Morning
DMZ tour
Half- or full-day guided tour to the Demilitarized Zone — the Third Infiltration Tunnel, Dora Observatory looking into North Korea, and the border stations.
+ Book ahead — passports required

The DMZ is only accessible on an authorised tour and popular dates sell out — reserve in advance. Bring your passports (checked at the border), and note the JSA/Panmunjom portion has stricter rules and sometimes closes, so confirm what your tour actually includes.

Late afternoon
Back to Seoul
Tours usually return mid-to-late afternoon — regroup, warm up and rest your feet.
Evening
Jjimjilbang — Korean spa
The quintessential Korean unwind: hot-spring baths, sauna rooms and a heated common floor. The perfect antidote to a cold day on your feet.
+ Where to go & how it works

Dragon Hill Spa (Yongsan) is the big, tourist-friendly classic. Baths are gender-separated and nude (shower first); in the shared sauna zone you wear the provided cotton pyjamas. Grab a sikhye (sweet rice drink) and a baked egg on the heated floor — that’s the ritual.

Dinner
Korean BBQFOOD
Grill-at-your-table night — samgyeopsal (pork belly) or marinated galbi, wrapped in lettuce with garlic, ssamjang and a shot of soju.
+ Do it right

Let the staff help with the grilling if it’s busy, and order the doenjang jjigae (soybean stew) + steamed egg on the side to round it out. Hongdae and Euljiro are full of buzzy, late-night BBQ joints.

Day 4 Wednesday, Dec 23 Markets, DDP & KTX to Busan
Morning
Gwangjang MarketFOOD
Seoul’s oldest market (1905) — the definitive Korean street-food hall. Come hungry, eat elbow-to-elbow at the stalls.
+ Don’t miss — the classics

Crispy bindaetteok (mung-bean pancake, ground fresh at the stall), the Netflix-famous mayak gimbap (“drug” mini rolls) with mustard-soy dip, yukhoe (seasoned raw beef), and hand-cut kalguksu. Wash it down with makgeolli.

+ Photo spot — the food-stall haze

The tightly-packed stalls under yellow lights, steam rising off the griddles, ajummas flipping pancakes — it’s one of the most atmospheric photo scenes in Seoul.

Late morning
Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP)
Zaha Hadid’s swooping silver spaceship of a building — a short walk from the market.
+ Photo spot — the curves & LED roses

Walk the exterior ramps for the flowing metallic-panel shots, and if the LED Rose Garden installation is running, the field of glowing roses against the building at dusk is stunning.

Afternoon
KTX to Busan
High-speed rail from Seoul Station down to Busan — about 2.5 hours, city centre to city centre.
+ Book your seats ahead

Reserve the KTX in advance (Korail app or at the station) — it’s the day before Christmas Eve, so trains fill up. Sit on the correct side and you may catch coastline glimpses near the end.

Evening
Arrive Busan — SeomyeonFOOD
Check in near Busan Station or Seomyeon (where the Gyeongju tours pick up), then head out for dinner.
+ Don’t miss — dwaeji gukbap

Busan’s signature dish is dwaeji gukbap — a hearty pork-and-rice soup you season yourself with chives, shrimp paste and chilli. There’s a whole “gukbap alley” near Seomyeon. Deeply warming after the train.

Day 5 Thursday, Dec 24 Gyeongju day tour
Morning
Gyeongju — UNESCO day tour
Guided day trip from Busan to Gyeongju, the ancient Silla-dynasty capital — often called “the museum without walls.”
+ Tour pickup

The GetYourGuide UNESCO Legacy tour picks up in Busan — another reason to stay near Busan Station / Seomyeon. Confirm the pickup point and time the night before.

Midday
Bulguksa Temple
8th-century hillside temple, a UNESCO World Heritage site — stone bridges, pagodas and beautifully painted eaves.
+ Photo spot — the stone stairways

Frame the temple’s twin stone staircase-bridges (Cheongungyo / Baegungyo) leading up to the gate, and the Dabotap & Seokgatap pagodas in the main courtyard.

Afternoon
Daereungwon & Hwangnidan-gilFOOD
The grassy royal burial mounds of Daereungwon, then the trendy cafe-and-shop street of Hwangnidan-gil right beside them.
+ Photo spot — the tumuli & lone tree

The rolling green burial mounds are surreal; the famous solitary tree (“Michu-wang tree”) framed against a mound is Gyeongju’s most-shared shot.

+ Don’t miss — Hwangnam-ppang

Grab a box of warm Hwangnam-ppang — Gyeongju’s famous thin red-bean cakes — and browse the aesthetic cafes lining Hwangnidan-gil.

Dusk
Donggung Palace & Wolji Pond
Reconstructed Silla royal pavilions beside a reflecting pond — the tour’s grand finale, best right as the lights come on.
+ Photo spot — the night reflection

This is one of Korea’s most beautiful night scenes: the illuminated pavilions mirrored in the still pond. Stay until it’s properly dark for the postcard reflection shot.

Day 6 Friday, Dec 25 Busan highlights · Christmas Day
Morning
Haedong Yonggungsa Temple
A rare Buddhist temple built right on the seaside cliffs — waves crashing below the shrines. Magical, and free.
+ Photo spot — temple over the sea

Shoot from the far side of the little cove looking back at the temple layered against the rocks and ocean. Go early to beat the crowds; the light is best in the morning.

Late morning
Gamcheon Culture Village
Pastel-painted refugee-era houses stacked up a hillside, now full of murals, tiny galleries and cafes — Busan’s “Santorini.”
+ Photo spot — the Little Prince

The Little Prince & the Fox statue overlooking the coloured rooftops is the iconic frame (expect a short queue). Wander the back alleys for quieter mural shots too.

+ Hidden gem — Huinyeoul Culture Village

If you have time, the nearby Huinyeoul coastal walk — white houses on a cliff above the sea, with a photogenic seaside tunnel — is far less touristy and gorgeous.

Afternoon
Haeundae Blueline Park
The coastal rail line reborn as a tourist ride — the open-air Beach Train and the pastel Sky Capsule gliding above the shoreline.
+ Photo spot — the Sky Capsule

The candy-coloured capsules on the elevated track over the sea are the shot — book the Sky Capsule ahead as it sells out. The Cheongsapo section with the twin lighthouses is especially pretty.

+ Hidden gem — Cheongsapo Daritdol Observatory

Near the Blueline route, this glass-floored skywalk juts out over the water — a quick, dramatic ocean view most tour groups skip.

Evening
Jagalchi MarketFOOD
Korea’s biggest fish market — pick your seafood downstairs and they’ll cook it for you upstairs, minutes fresh.
+ Don’t miss — how it works

Choose from the tanks (hwe / sashimi, grilled fish, king crab, or grilled jangeo eel), agree a price, then take it upstairs to be prepared. Nearby BIFF Square has the famous ssiat hotteok — seed-stuffed fried sweet pancakes — for dessert.

Day 7 Saturday, Dec 26 Busan art & back to Seoul
Morning
F1963
A 1960s wire-rope factory reborn as an arts complex — galleries, a design bookshop, a craft brewery and a beautiful bamboo courtyard cafe.
+ Alternative — Busan Museum of Art

If you’d rather a proper gallery, the Busan Museum of Art (with its Space Lee Ufan annex) is right by Haeundae/BEXCO — a calm, quality art stop before the train.

Afternoon
KTX back to Seoul
Return high-speed train up to Seoul — ~2.5 hrs. Check back into your Seoul base for the last stretch of the trip.
Evening
MMCA Seoul
The National Museum of Modern & Contemporary Art, next to Gyeongbokgung — open late on Saturdays (often free evening entry), so it’s a great arrival-night stop.
+ Hidden gem — the neighbourhood after

The museum sits in Samcheong-dong / Bukchon — wander the surrounding alleys for cosy wine bars and dinner spots to ease back into Seoul.

Day 8 Sunday, Dec 27 Nami, alpacas & morning calm
Full day
Countryside day tour
A classic Seoul day trip east into Gangwon: Alpaca World, Nami Island, the Garden of Morning Calm, and rail-bike / Eobi Ice Valley depending on the tour.
+ Photo spot — Nami’s tree-lined avenue

Nami Island’s dead-straight Metasequoia Lane (of Winter Sonata fame) is the money shot — especially dusted with snow. Feed the ostriches and squirrels while you’re there.

+ Hidden gem — the Lighting Festival

The Garden of Morning Calm runs its Lighting Festival through winter — hundreds of thousands of lights blanketing the garden after dark. If your tour includes the evening, this is the highlight; if not, it’s worth checking the timing.

Day 9 Monday, Dec 28 Art, books & a slow day
Afternoon
Starfield Library (COEX)
The giant open library inside the COEX Mall in Gangnam — two-storey shelves and a soaring atrium. Free to walk through.
+ Photo spot — the towering shelves

Shoot from the lower floor looking up at the 13m bookshelves and hanging lights. It’s busy — a weekday afternoon is your best shot at a clean frame.

Later
Leeum Museum of Art
Samsung’s superb private museum in Itaewon — traditional Korean art and blue-chip contemporary work in buildings by three star architects. (Closed Mondays — swap to another day if needed.)
+ Hidden gem — the neighbourhood

Leeum sits above Itaewon / Hannam-dong, one of Seoul’s most design-forward areas — flagship concept stores, indie coffee and good dinner nearby.

Evening
Free eveningFOOD
A relaxed dinner — a good night for something you haven’t had yet.
+ Don’t miss — jjimdak or naengmyeon

Try Andong jjimdak (braised soy-glazed chicken with glass noodles) to share, or go regional with a bossam (boiled pork with kimchi wraps). Hongdae and Gangnam are full of options.

Day 10 Tuesday, Dec 29 Last full day in Seoul
Daytime
Insadong & around
Traditional crafts, tea houses, galleries and souvenir lanes — the place to pick up last gifts (ceramics, stationery, hanji paper).
+ Hidden gem — a traditional tea house

Duck into one of Insadong’s tucked-away tea houses for citron (yuja) or ginger tea — a warm, quiet break from the cold and the crowds.

Afternoon
MMCA or one more museum
If you skipped it earlier, circle back to MMCA Seoul — or pick whichever art stop you missed.
Evening
Euljiro & farewell dinnerFOOD
Wrap the trip in Euljiro (Hip-jiro) — retro machine-shop alleys hiding some of Seoul’s coolest bars and restaurants.
+ Hidden gem — the hidden bars

Euljiro is a maze of unmarked doors and rooftop bars above old print shops — part of the fun is finding one. A memorable last night out.

+ Don’t miss — a final feast

Go all-in one last time: sizzling galbi, a bubbling budae jjigae (army stew), or fried chicken & beer (chimaek) — the perfect Korean send-off.

Day 11 Wednesday, Dec 30 Departure
Morning
Last-minute Seoul
Hangout, do last minute things
+ One for the road

Grab a warm hotteok or a gyeranppang from a street cart, and stock up on snacks (Market Kurly / a convenience store) for the flight.

Afternoon
Head to Incheon
Leave with a comfortable buffer for the 7:40 pm flight — the AREX or a taxi to ICN, and arrive ~3 hours ahead for an international departure.
+ At the airport

ICN Terminal 1 & 2 both have a Traditional Korean Cultural Experience zone and plenty of last-minute food and duty-free — leave a little extra time to enjoy the airport itself.
