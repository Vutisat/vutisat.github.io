Helping whales and fishers share the sea.

Currently Sub Sea Sonics · Trap Timer

A single rope from a fishing trap to its buoy can stand in the water for days. For a whale, that line is a trap of its own. Sub Sea Sonics builds on-demand fishing gear: the rope and float stay down with the trap and rise only when the fisher comes to haul.

What I build

Without a buoy at the surface, nobody can see where the gear is. Trap Timer is the software that makes on-demand gear workable on the water: GPS gear marking in place of the buoy, set and haul logging, and a shared map that warns fishers before they set near someone else's traps on the seafloor, with location data available to regulators where a fishery requires it.

I took over the codebase with a friend after its original developer retired, and I now lead most of its new feature development.

From prototype to the platform bankers market deals with.

Datasite · Senior Software Engineer · 2018–2021

Before a company is sold, its bankers have to find the right buyers, usually from a spreadsheet. Datasite Outreach replaced the spreadsheet: one place to build the buyer list, send confidential material, track every buyer's interest and move the serious ones into due diligence.

What I built

I was one of the lead engineers who took Outreach from ideas and a working prototype to a launched product, used by bankers marketing deals worth billions of dollars. I built its GraphQL, Node and Spring Boot APIs and the data integration with Salesforce.

Then I made it ready for production: monitoring and alerts, training the application support teams, and making sure the system scaled as customers arrived.

What it does

  • Bulk email to the whole buyer list at once, without copy-paste errors.
  • Watermarked documents, so confidential files stay traceable.
  • A live buyer tracker in place of the spreadsheet.
  • A direct path for the serious buyers into the deal's data room.

Target · Software Engineer · 2016–2018

A single product page needs data owned by many teams: the item, its price, its reviews, whether your store has it and where it sits on the shelf. Redsky is the layer that gathers it all and serves it, fast, to Target.com and the Target apps.

What I built

I built Redsky's real-time in-store pricing: the price for the store you've chosen, live on every page that asks, at more than ten million requests a day.

I also stood up the API that lets the Target apps show where an item is in the store, so a shopper can walk straight to it.

The platform it grew into

Redsky went on to consolidate close to 100 separate APIs into one GraphQL-based platform: 200 APIs for about 30 client teams, 250 schema types, more than 50 integrations underneath.
