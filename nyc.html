M

Museums

5 stops
Free Loads of museums are free or pay-what-you-wish on certain days. Check nycforfree.co for the current schedule before you buy a ticket.

Art

MoMAMidtownTop pick

One of the most prestigious art museums in the world, and my first stop for any art lover. Don't miss Starry Night, Melted Clock, Monet's room

The MetMuseum Mile

Go here if you want art with a bit more history and artifacts in the mix. While you're up there, walk through Central Park's Conservatory Garden and past the exterior of the Guggenheim.

Salon 94Upper East SideHidden gem

A small free gallery right by the Guggenheim. Usually something cool inside and easy to miss, so pop in while you're in the neighborhood.

Not Art

American Museum of Natural HistoryUpper West Side

World-class, and great whether you've got kids or not. Highlights: the top floor full of dinosaur fossils, the Hall of Gems and Minerals (my favorite), the ocean room with the giant whale, and a planetarium with a great 5-minute show. You could spend a whole day here, so pick and choose.

Statue of Liberty & Ellis IslandBattery Park ferry

Half a day, well spent. I put this off for years thinking it was too touristy, but it's genuinely neat. The real surprise is the Ellis Island Museum of Immigration, which is beautifully done. One ticket covers both islands (Manhattan, then Liberty, then Ellis, then back). If you only want to see the Statue from the water, the Staten Island Ferry does it for free.

R

Walking Routes

3 routes + 1 zone

The best way to see the city is on foot. Here are three routes I'd actually walk, stop by stop.

A

West Side

Hudson Yards → Little Island
  • Hudson Yards & The Vessel

    Start here.

  • The High Line

    Walk south down this old elevated railroad that's been turned into a park.

  • Chelsea Market

    A great food hall. The Thai spot is good, and the long line at Los Tacos No. 1 is worth it (get the al pastor).

  • Little Island

    A few minutes further, a small park floating out on the Hudson.

B

Midtown

Times Square → Bryant Park
  • Times Square

    Start here, then walk over.

  • Rockefeller Center

    Pass Radio City Music Hall on the way. The best view of Rockefeller is from Fifth Avenue.

  • Fifth Avenue

    Where to go if you're up for some high-end shopping.

  • Grand Central Terminal

    A short walk, and a beautiful building worth seeing. Bathrooms are in the basement.

  • New York Public Library

    Right nearby. Book a tour ahead to see the Rose Reading Room. Bryant Park is right behind it.

C

Brooklyn

Brooklyn Bridge → DUMBO
  • Brooklyn Bridge

    Start near Chinatown and walk across.

  • DUMBO

    Don't miss the iconic photo spot under the bridge (Google "DUMBO Washington Street").

  • Main Street Park

    Ride the carousel and catch some of the best skyline views in the city. There's a food hall nearby too.

  • Randolph Beer

    Great view of the city, a big beer selection, and games if you're up for a drink.

SoHo & the Village

Lower Manhattan · wander, don't plan

  • SoHo / Prince St. Culture, influencers, the occasional celeb sighting. Where the cool kids hang out.
  • Washington Square Park My favorite. The best glimpse of the city's energy and the mix of people who make it.
  • Uncommons A great board game café right by the park.

Know Before You Go

5 tips

01 Tap to pay

Just tap your card or phone on the subway turnstile or bus reader. Easiest way to get around, no MetroCard needed.

02 Wear good shoes

Expect to walk a lot. Comfortable shoes make or break the day.

03 Jaywalking is legal

It's officially allowed here now, so cross like a local (just look both ways).

04 Watch for scams

If someone asks for money or "donations," especially around Times Square or Rockefeller, it's almost always a scam. That includes the fake monks.

05 Don't block the sidewalk

Step to the side if you need to check your phone or look at a map. New Yorkers walk with purpose.
