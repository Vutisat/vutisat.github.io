Pob's Thailand recs.


BEFORE YOU GO
=============

When to go
----------
- The two coasts have opposite rainy seasons. Roughly: Andaman is best Nov-Apr,
  the Gulf side holds up better Jun-Sep. Don't plan both coasts in one trip blindly.
- Burning season in the north, roughly Feb-Apr: agricultural smoke, genuinely bad air
  in Chiang Mai/Chiang Rai. This is the single biggest planning trap for the north
  and it overlaps with "high season." Check AQI before booking those dates.
- Cool season (Nov-Feb) is the pleasant window nationwide and the most expensive.
- Songkran, Apr 13-15 - nationwide water fight. Either plan around it or into it.
- Loy Krathong / Yi Peng, November - lanterns, biggest in Chiang Mai. Date moves yearly.

Logistics
---------
- Use GRAB app to order ride share or food, very helpful to have when in Thailand
- Tourist SIM/eSIM at the airport is cheap and worth it for Grab and maps.
- Overnight sleeper trains (Bangkok-Chiang Mai, Bangkok-Surat Thani) are a real option
  and book out early.
- National parks often have a foreigner price. Bring cash; cards are unreliable there.
- Verify the current visa-free stay length for a US passport before the trip -
  it has changed more than once recently.


THE NORTH
=========

Northern Thailand consist of many moutains and forests.
It's easy to spend the night at the hotel in Chaing Mai or Chaing Rai because those are the two major cities.
However if you can find a legitimate places near pu chi fa, I highly recommend it.

These are highly recommended the places to visit(Please image google them and see if it fits you)

- Phu Chi Fa
You'd have to be there the night before the morning if you want to see the beatiful sunrise(try to stay there a night if you can)... What it is, is that you go so high up the moutains that you're above the clouds... and when the sunrise it's above the a sea of clouds.
   http://www.hoteltrip.com/travelblogs/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/Phu-Chi-Fa-Chiang-Rai.jpg

- Doi Mae Salong, Chiang Rai — Yunnanese Chinese hill town, tea plantations. Close
     enough to pair with Phu Chi Fa / Golden Triangle.

- Doi Tung
Another famous tourist place... There's a garden to celebrate the King's mother that is very pretty... (See link below).
   http://i.ytimg.com/vi/AjzeEb7jPSs/maxresdefault.jpg

- Doi Inthanon
Doi means moutain peak... this is another moutain if you have time is cool to visit... it's the highest point in Thailand. Near the top there are two huge "jaydee" built for the King's and the Queen's 60th birthdays. Somewhat recommended if you have time but not the biggest thing.
   http://images.summitpost.org/original/610604.JPG

- Wat Rong Khun
Famous wat(buddhist temple) for both thai and foreigners tourists... I highly recommend it, it's beautifully built...

- Golden Triangle
The triangle is an island that's serparted in to Burma, Laos and Thailand. If you want to visit Laos or Burma from thailand this would be a neat little plave to do so... plenty of tourists therefore plenty of accomendation, it's actually pretty nice so don't let tourists discourage you.

- Baan Dam (the "Black House"), Chiang Rai — the dark counterpart to Wat Rong Khun,
  by a different Chiang Rai artist. Usually done as a pair with the White Temple.

CENTRAL
=======

Bangkok
-------
Bangkok, go to Siam.
Explore Siam Paragon, walk down to Central World... around it (less interesting) are Siam Discovery, Siam center <--younstersscene
You think Mall of America is big, both Siam paragon and Central world is bigger... and they're a block from each other.

Go to all the famous temples, especially Wat Phra Kaew. Also beauitful at Night.
- Wat Arun at sunset from the Thonburi side, Wat Pho for the reclining Buddha.
  Both pair with Wat Phra Kaew in one river day.

Other things in the city:
- Yaowarat (Chinatown) after dark - the street food stretch.
- Jim Thompson House - old Thai teak houses, silk, and the disappearance story.
- MOCA nice modern art museum with very interesting Thai art
- Take a boat from ICON Siam at night around the city, amazing views.

Day trips out of Bangkok
------------------------
- An hour south of Bangkok you can go to the floating market.
  Amphawa Floating Market is the one I recommend, livliest with most things to see. EXTREMELY crowded though.
- Maeklong Railway Market + Amphawa - the market that folds up when the train passes through.
  Usually bundled with an Amphawa trip which is the famous floating market. Both can be done in a day trip and I recommend it.
- Floating market alternatives if Amphawa is too crowded: Tha Kha (smaller, more local)
  or Khlong Lat Mayom (inside Bangkok, food-focused).
- Ayutthaya - hour+ from Bangkok, easy day trip by train or tour. Ruined capital which still is very unique and nice. Be aware it'll be hot in the sun.
- Khao Yai National Park - closest real national park to Bangkok, wildlife + waterfalls.


THE SOUTH
=========

The most beautiful beaches are in Trang/Krabi or really any parts of the "Andaman Coast" ... there are a ton of beaches and islands(called Koh/Ko)...

The two coasts are different trips - see the rainy-season note up top before picking one.

Andaman side (west coast)
-------------------------
-Phuket is highly discouraged, it's a tourist trap with a huge party scene...
Nothing is authentically Thai and the beaches are subpar
unless you're simply looking to party, raves, etc.

-Koh Lanta is also very popular among tourist... but it's actually quite beautiful. Many ranges of places to stay from luxurious hotels to cheaper small rooms.
From there you can take day tours to see waterfalls(depends on the season, best during rainy), four/three island tours
if you take the tour make sure you get to go to "Koh Rok"
^one of the most beautiful beaches availible, you can camp there too... barely any buildings, well preserved.
^snorkling and diving tours will make you feel like you're in a different world, I swam with school of thousands of fishes
^cave tour is cool
^Google Koh Mook, it's a green emerald beach inside a cave... amazing.

-Koh Phi phi
^Another popular one but still good
^A bit more higher class, well developed tourist place

-ao phang nga national park
if you're interested in see Koh tapu aka James Bond island (famous island from The Man with the Golden Gun)

-Khao Sok NP - not an island: Cheow Lan lake, floating raft houses under limestone karsts.
Often the best-rated non-beach thing in the south. Look up images, book ahead of time!!

Gulf side (east coast)
----------------------
-Koh Samui - Easy access but gets very busy

-Koh Tao - the cheap-diving island. Busy, but that's where the certifications happen.

-Ang Thong Marine Park - the archipelago in the Koh Samui/Phangan cluster.

Either coast
------------
Other small island might be pretty but could be pretty remote and not convenient, more adventurous than relaxing... Plenty of islands have camp grounds


BIG DON'TS IN THAILAND
======================
1)Touch anyone's head/hair
2)Step on money or books (feet are consider lower objects and disrespectful)
3)Criticize the monarch (literally illegal)
4)Touch any buddhist sculpture
5) be unaware of your possesion

DO
==
1)Put your wallet in front pocket. Trust me, even if you think you are sensitive... backpocket is big nono, no one does it.
2)Pause during national anthem (take hats off) happens every 6pm.
3)Cover shoulders and knees at temples, shoes off. Enforced at the big ones.

GAPS IN THE CURRENT DOC
- Isaan (the northeast) isn't mentioned at all. It's the least touristed third of
  the country and has the Khmer ruins and the Mekong.
- The Gulf coast / eastern islands aren't mentioned — only the Andaman side.
- Ayutthaya and Sukhothai (the two old capitals) aren't in here.
- Kanchanaburi / the western border isn't in here.
- Bangkok section is Siam + temples only; no food, no river, no neighborhoods.
- No section on *when* to go, which matters more in Thailand than most places.

NORTH — possible additions


- Chiang Dao, north of Chiang Mai — limestone massif and cave temple, much quieter
  than Pai. Day trip or one night.
- Mae Hong Son loop — multi-day drive/ride out of Chiang Mai. Ban Rak Thai (Yunnanese
  village on the Burma border) and the bamboo bridge at Su Tong Pae are the usual stops.
  Only worth it with your own transport and 3+ days.
- Nan province — far east, murals at Wat Phumin, very little foreign tourism.
  Hard to reach, which is the point.
- Wat Chalermphrakiat, Lampang — small chedis on bare limestone spires, climbed by stair.
  Very photogenic, not on most itineraries.
- Wat Phra That Lampang Luang, Lampang — one of the oldest surviving wooden Lanna temples.
- Sukhothai Historical Park (+ Si Satchanalai) — the pre-Ayutthaya capital. Bike it at
  dawn. Often rated above Ayutthaya for atmosphere.

NORTHEAST / ISAAN — the biggest hole in the doc
- Phanom Rung, Buriram — Khmer temple on an extinct volcano rim. The big one.
  Prasat Hin Phimai (Nakhon Ratchasima) is the other, and they pair well.
- Wat Phu Tok, Bueng Kan — wooden walkways bolted around a sandstone tower.
  Remote. Probably the most dramatic thing in the region.
- Sam Phan Bok, Ubon Ratchathani — eroded rock basins in the Mekong bed.
  Dry season only (roughly Jan–May); underwater the rest of the year.
- Pha Taem, Ubon Ratchathani — prehistoric cliff paintings over the Mekong.
- Chiang Khan, Loei — old riverside teak shophouse street on the Mekong.
  Now popular with Thai weekenders, so go midweek.
- Phu Kradueng, Loei — plateau you hike up; camping on top. Closed part of the year,
  check before committing.
- Food angle: Isaan is where som tam / laab / grilled stuff actually comes from.

CENTRAL & WEST — possible additions
- Kanchanaburi — Erawan Falls (seven tiers, swimmable), the Death Railway and
  Hellfire Pass, and the WWII history. Sangkhlaburi further out has the long Mon
  wooden bridge and a very different (Mon/Karen) feel.
- Phetchaburi — Phra Nakhon Khiri hilltop palace and the Khao Luang cave shrine.
  Skipped by nearly everyone driving south.


SOUTH — islands not already in the doc
Andaman side (same coast Pob already recommends):
- Koh Kradan and Koh Libong, Trang — the quiet neighbors to Koh Mook.
- Koh Yao Noi / Koh Yao Yai, in Phang Nga Bay — Muslim fishing villages, right between
  Phuket and Krabi but nothing like either.
- Railay / Tonsai, Krabi — boat access only; world-class limestone climbing.
- Koh Lipe + Tarutao NP, Satun — far south, very clear water. Lipe has gotten busy.
- Khao Sok NP — not an island: Cheow Lan lake, floating raft houses under limestone karsts.
  Often the best-rated non-beach thing in the south.
Gulf / eastern side (absent from the doc entirely, opposite rainy season):
- Koh Kood and Koh Mak, Trat — near the Cambodian border, far less developed than
  anything in the Andaman. Probably the closest match to what Pob likes.
- Koh Tao — the cheap-diving island. Busy, but that's where the certifications happen.
- Ang Thong Marine Park — the archipelago in the Koh Samui/Phangan cluster.
